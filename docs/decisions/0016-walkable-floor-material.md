# 0016 — Separate walkable-floor material from blocked ground

**Status:** accepted on 2026-08-23

## Problem

The generated map originally repeated complete facade cells over every blocked tile. Removing those cells made collision invisible. Reintroducing mixed floor cells exposed the topology but produced a checkerboard, and four-sided stone outlines read like a debug overlay. A runtime Canvas brightness filter made deterministic capture unacceptably slow and would have harmed animation smoothness.

## Decision

Use two authored raster materials at one cell scale:

- `environment-floor.png` is a fitted flagstone base drawn across every map cell with contiguous atlas coordinates.
- `environment-ground.png` is a translucent rugged overlay on blocked cells, never a differently scaled substitute beneath a walkable room.
- A low-opacity, sprite-backed masonry strip remains at blocked/walkable transitions as a secondary collision cue.

Both 1024 × 1024 materials are divided into a deterministic 16 × 16 grid and addressed by map coordinates. Adjacent draw calls therefore use adjacent source cells instead of random variants. The blocked overlay is graded from 0.34 at a cardinal walkable edge to 0.62 in deep blocked space; an additional floor fringe softens the transition. The full-map ground image remains only as a 0.22-opacity underlay for sub-pixel canvas gaps. No per-frame pixel filter is required.

The committed source is `art/source/environment/floor-source.png`. The deterministic atlas builder resizes it to 1024 × 1024 and records its output hash in `build-manifest.json`.

## Image-generation record

The floor was generated by editing against `art/source/environment/ground-source.png` as the style reference. The prompt requested a seamless, top-down, dark-gothic action-RPG floor; fitted ancient flagstone; slightly lighter and more ordered than the rocky ground; restrained warm ember and cold cyan traces; uniform density; and explicitly forbade walls, buildings, props, actors, text, symbols, UI, grid lines, isolated tiles, and a perspective horizon.

The first image-generator result exposed a broad orange-left/cyan-right gradient when addressed by map tiles and was rejected. A reference-guided revision explicitly removed directional lighting and large color blocks, balanced rare ember/mineral traces locally, and required continuous stones and cracks across opposite edges. The generator returned a 1254 × 1254 PNG. That unmodified second output is the committed source. The normal Sharp-based atlas build performs the reproducible 1024 × 1024 normalization; no hand painting or runtime image manipulation is applied.

Runtime texture addressing uses a 16 × 16 grid, so a material does not repeat until 16 logical tiles rather than the rejected four-tile period. The first runtime composition still failed visual inspection: it drew walkable fragments over a stretched blocked-ground image, exposing a hard map-sized square and two incompatible rock scales. It was rejected even though the source-material validator passed. The replacement draws the flagstone base everywhere and layers blocked material at the same scale. Blocked cells next to walkable space receive a low-opacity floor-material fringe and a masonry edge sprite; together they soften the transition while preserving collision topology.

## Quality contribution

The source validator measures regional luminance/chroma drift and wrap seams, and proves itself with injected broad-gradient and edge-seam images. The scene contract requires the scale-matched floor base on every map cell, a blocked overlay only on collision cells, and a sprite boundary plus blend at every blocked-to-walkable edge. A browser assessor samples the actual composed canvas: collision contrast must remain perceptible while same-material tile seams stay below the declared median threshold. Injected invisible-boundary and exaggerated-seam evidence must fail by name. Independent visual review still has veto power over repetition, artificial outlines, perspective, and coherence with structures and actors.
